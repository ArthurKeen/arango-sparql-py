### Locked Decisions

**Growth target & statistical power:**
- **D-01: Fix MDE, derive N.** Target locked as **MDE ≤ 5pt**. Required held-out case
  count **N is derived from a power analysis**, not guessed. Use paired McNemar (same
  test family Phase 07 pre-registered), computed at the observed baseline pass-rate,
  driven by the discordant-pair proportion. The researcher/planner runs the power calc
  and states the assumptions; the corpus grows to the resulting N.
- **D-02: No hard case-count cap** — grow until MDE ≤ 5pt is met. Human curation is the
  cost driver, but removing the measurement ceiling is the whole point of the phase, so
  accept the curation cost rather than truncating. Expected landing zone is the low
  hundreds; the exact figure is D-01's output.
- **D-03: Rebalance the difficulty mix toward failing classes for headroom.** Over-weight
  classes where models actually fail (hard property paths, multi-hop,
  negative/`expect_refusal`) rather than scaling today's mix proportionally.

**Synthesis mechanism:**
- **D-04: Hybrid generation — templated gold + LLM natural language.** Enumerate
  gold-SPARQL shapes deterministically over the ontology, then have an LLM write the
  natural-language question for each templated gold. Human-curated.
- **D-05: Machine-generated golds only** — the LLM never authors the gold. Every gold
  parses (rdflib) and transpiles by construction.
- **D-06: Committed, seeded generator.** Checked in, deterministic (pinned seed),
  regenerable, auditable. The LLM NL step runs gated/offline (like the live sweep, not
  CI); its outputs are committed as trusted repo data.

**Ontology surface:**
- **D-07: Extend the eval fixture ontology into a CDF-shaped, self-contained conceptual
  schema.** Grow the ~7-term fixture into a larger, human-readable OWL/RDFS schema
  (more entities + datatype attributes + object-property edges + typed collections)
  resembling the shape CDF / Onto-Extract emit. **Scope guard — self-contained, NOT
  coupled to live CDF**: stays a self-contained Turtle block inside the eval fixtures,
  parsed only by `SchemaResolver.from_turtle`. Not wired to the live evolving CDF
  ontology, not grounded in a specific customer domain.
- **D-08: Stay within proven `phys` idioms only.** New terms use exclusively: datatype
  property → attribute, object property → edge collection (`phys:edgeCollectionName`),
  typed collection (`phys:collectionName`/`phys:typeField`/`phys:typeValue`). Every gold
  transpiles with **zero transpiler work**.

**Curation & gold validity:**
- **D-09: English-only faithfulness curation** (curator is not SPARQL-fluent). The
  generator emits a canonical plain-English **gloss** of each gold. Faithfulness review
  is English-to-English: does the LLM's natural question mean the same as the gloss? An
  automated LLM-judge pre-screens; the human confirms/rejects in plain English. Gold
  *validity* is guaranteed separately by the deterministic parse+transpile gate.

**Disjointness at scale:**
- **D-10: Bank↔corpus disjointness holds by construction.** Partition the generation
  space so the eval corpus and the few-shot bank draw from structurally disjoint slices
  (different entity subsets / template families). The committed 3-way gate
  (`test_fewshot_bank_disjoint.py`) stays the backstop, not the primary mechanism.

**Baseline scope:**
- **D-11: Regenerate scripted in-phase; live re-baseline is human-run, documented.**
  Regenerate scripted `baseline.json` + headroom invariant in-phase (deterministic, CI).
  Pin N from the D-01 power analysis under stated assumptions. Prepare the live
  re-baseline (configs + runbook); credentialed human runs it out-of-band.

## Summary

This phase has one central deliverable the CONTEXT explicitly calls out: **derive N,
don't guess it.** The research confirms Phase 07's own McNemar helpers
(`runner.paired_mcnemar`) are the right instrument, and standard McNemar sample-size
theory (Connor 1987, and the simplified form used by most power-analysis tooling) gives
a closed-form answer once a discordant-pair-rate assumption is stated. Applying that
formula to Phase 07's own observed data (b/c counts from the dense/bm25/gpt-5 sweeps,
discordant proportions 0.08–0.28 across four comparisons) produces a **materially larger
N than the CONTEXT's "low hundreds" expectation** — the honest range is roughly
**470–950 held-out cases** depending on the assumed discordant rate, with **≈700–800**
as the best point estimate given the observed data. This is the single most important
finding of this research and is flagged prominently for user confirmation (it directly
gates D-02's "no hard cap" cost/curation tradeoff).

The mechanics side is comparatively lower-risk: the existing harness (`SchemaResolver`,
`_canonical`/alpha-equivalence judge, the 3-way disjointness gate, the gold-transpilable
gate) needs **zero changes** — corpus growth is new data plus a new generator module plus
new/extended tests, not harness surgery. The ontology-driven-synthesis literature (§3 of
the designated research input) directly supports the D-04/05/06 hybrid design: FlexKBQA
and the 1-hop ontology-extension paper (15→~513 pairs, ~34×) are close analogs to what
this phase does (25→~700-800, ~28-32×) — a validating parallel worth citing in the plan.
The one genuinely new design problem is the **faithful-gloss mechanism** for D-09
(non-SPARQL-fluent curation at scale) — this research proposes a template-paired gloss
(generated from the same slot-fill data as the gold, never by reading the SPARQL back)
as the soundest construction, since it removes an entire class of "gloss doesn't match
gold" bugs by never round-tripping through SPARQL syntax.

A second material risk surfaced during research (not present in the CONTEXT): **extending
the ontology can silently invalidate existing `expect_refusal` negatives** whose rationale
text asserts a domain concept is absent. This is flagged as Pitfall 1 with a concrete
mitigation (avoid re-using vocabulary — "amount", "employment/salary/department" — already
claimed absent by `neg-absent-invoice-class`/`neg-unexpressible-salary-relation`; audit all
three existing negatives against the extended schema before the phase closes).

**Primary recommendation:** Target **N ≈ 700-800** held-out corpus cases (plus a
proportionally-sized, disjoint few-shot bank), derived via the pure-Python power-calc
function proposed below at α=0.05 two-sided, power=0.80, assumed discordant proportion
π≈0.20-0.25 (the range actually observed in Phase 07's own sweeps) — explicitly flagged
as an assumption requiring user sign-off, not a locked fact. Extend the ontology with 3
new classes (`:Product`, `:Category`, `:Review`) and their attributes/edges, staying
strictly within the 3 proven `phys` idioms already exercised by `bgp_select.yml` /
`edge_traversal.yml` / `hybrid.yml`. Build the generator as deterministic template
enumeration + seeded partition-and-split (never post-hoc filtering) for D-10
disjointness, and a template-paired gloss (never SPARQL-derived) for D-09 faithfulness.

### Pattern 1: Power analysis — deriving N from MDE (THE central deliverable)

**What:** A closed-form sample-size calculation for a paired McNemar test, using the
classic approximate formula (Connor 1987; the same family cited by
powerandsamplesize.com/pwrss/PASS documentation):

```
n = (z_{α/2} + z_β)² · π / δ²
```

where `π` = assumed proportion of discordant pairs (`b+c` over `N`), `δ` = the minimum
detectable effect (the paired pass-rate delta you want to be able to detect, e.g. 0.05
for 5pt), `z_{α/2}` = 1.96 for α=0.05 two-sided, `z_β` = 0.8416 for 80% power (0.5244 for
70%, 1.2816 for 90%).

**When to use:** At phase-planning time, to size the corpus BEFORE generation, and again
empirically once real per-arm case data exists (compute the *achieved* MDE at whatever
`N`/`π` the corpus has actually reached — the inverse of the same formula) — this
supports D-02's "grow until MDE ≤ 5pt is met" without committing blindly to one static N.

**The verified math, applied to this repo's own data (Phase 07 sweep, `baseline.json`
`phase07_dense_few_shot_sweep`):**

| Comparison | b | c | n=b+c | N (cases) | π = n/N | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gpt-4o-mini dense-vs-zero | 5 | 2 | 7 | 25 | 0.28 | primary confirmatory (p=0.453, null) |
| gpt-4o-mini bm25-vs-zero | 6 | 0 | 6 | 25 | 0.24 | secondary (p=0.031, significant) |
| gpt-5-mini dense-vs-zero | 1 | 1 | 2 | 25 | 0.08 | exploratory (near-ceiling on both arms) |
| gpt-5 dense-vs-zero | 5 | 1 | 6 | 25 | 0.24 | exploratory (p=0.219, null but +16pt point estimate) |

Observed discordant proportions range **0.08–0.28**, with three of four comparisons
clustering at **0.24–0.28**. Using this repo's own data (not an external default) as the
planning assumption:

**Sensitivity table** (α=0.05 two-sided, power=0.80, `z_{α/2}+z_β`=2.8016 → coefficient
7.849; δ=0.05 → δ²=0.0025):

| Assumed π (discordant rate) | Required N (power=0.80) | Required N (power=0.90) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 314 | 421 |
| 0.15 | 471 | 631 |
| **0.20 (optimistic — post-rebalancing)** | **628** | 841 |
| **0.25 (matches 3/4 observed comparisons)** | **785** | 1,051 |
| 0.30 | 942 | 1,261 |
| 0.35 | 1,099 | 1,471 |

**Recommendation:** target **N ≈ 700–800** (splitting the difference between the π=0.20
optimistic case and the π=0.25 case that matches most of the observed data), at 80%
power. This is presented as the D-01 output with full assumption disclosure — see
Assumptions Log A1. **This is materially larger than the CONTEXT's "low hundreds"
expectation** and should be confirmed with the user before the plan commits engineering/
curation effort to a specific N; see Open Question 1.

**Why a HIGHER discordant rate does NOT mean less N is needed (a common misreading):**
The formula's numerator grows with π for a *fixed* target δ — more "noise" (pairs that
flip in both directions) requires more total data to detect a fixed net signal, not less.
What actually matters for power is (a) enough headroom that π is bounded away from 0 at
all (a corpus at ceiling or floor has zero discordant potential — see the gpt-5-mini
case above, π=0.08, which is why that comparison had essentially no power), and (b) N.
D-03's rebalancing toward failing classes exists to guarantee (a), not to "maximize π
for its own sake" — over-concentrating on classes that fail 100% of the time under EVERY
arm (zero AND intervention) is equally powerless, since a case that's concordant-fail
contributes nothing. The target rebalancing should aim for classes with **baseline pass
rates roughly in the 20-70% band** (meaningful failure AND meaningful potential to flip),
not classes at 0% or 100% baseline pass rate.

**Citation:** Connor, R.J. (1987). "Sample size for testing differences in proportions
for the paired-sample design." *Biometrics* 43(1):207-211. [CITED: confirmed via
WebSearch against powerandsamplesize.com/PASS/pwrss documentation summaries of the
Connor formula family — the exact paper was not directly fetched, so the citation itself
is MEDIUM confidence; the arithmetic derivation above was independently verified by
hand against the repo's own `paired_mcnemar` exact-test semantics.]

### Pattern 3: Faithful gloss generation — template-paired, never SPARQL-derived (D-09)

**What:** Every template family is authored as a **pair** of Python string templates
sharing one slot-fill dict — a SPARQL template AND an English gloss template — so the
gloss is generated from the SAME structured data as the gold, never by re-reading the
SPARQL text:

```python
# Source: proposed pattern, not yet in codebase — mirrors the existing
# corpus.yml authoring convention of pairing `nl:`/`expected:` by hand,
# but makes the pairing MECHANICAL via a shared slot-fill dict.
@dataclass
class TemplateFamily:
    name: str                      # e.g. "count_per_key"
    difficulty_class: str          # e.g. "aggregation"
    sparql_template: str           # "SELECT ?k (COUNT(?x) AS ?c) WHERE {{ ?k a :{cls} ; :{edge} ?x . }} GROUP BY ?k"
    gloss_template: str            # "Count the number of {x_label} for each {k_label}."

def instantiate(family: TemplateFamily, slots: dict) -> tuple[str, str]:
    """Returns (gold_sparql, gloss) built from the IDENTICAL slot dict —
    the gloss can never drift from the gold's actual structure because
    both are string.format()-filled from one source of truth."""
    return family.sparql_template.format(**slots), family.gloss_template.format(**slots)
```

The gated LLM NL-authoring step then takes ONLY the gloss (never the SPARQL) as input:
`"Rephrase this as a natural user question, varied phrasing, same meaning: {gloss}"`.
The LLM-judge faithfulness pre-screen compares the LLM's question against the gloss
(English-to-English) and must explicitly check **both** directions — added detail not in
the gloss, AND dropped constraints from the gloss — a common single-direction-only judge
failure mode (see Pitfall 4).

**When to use:** For every template family, author the gloss template alongside the
SPARQL template at generator-authoring time (a one-time human cost, same cost class as
authoring today's hand-written `nl:`/`expected:` pairs) — never attempt to auto-derive a
gloss from parsed SPARQL algebra after the fact.

### Pattern 4: Deterministic disjoint partitioning (D-10)

**What:** Enumerate the full template × entity/slot-fill cross-product with a pinned
seed, dedup by canonical-algebra **skeleton** (reusing `test_fewshot_bank_disjoint.py`'s
existing `_skeleton()` — literals/URIs abstracted) to drop near-duplicates, THEN split
the deduped set into two disjoint pools via a seeded shuffle:

```python
# Source: proposed pattern extending runner.py's existing bootstrap
# seeding convention (seed=1234 in bootstrap_paired_delta).
import random

def partition(unique_instances: list[GeneratedCase], seed: int, bank_fraction: float = 0.25) -> tuple[list, list]:
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    shuffled = unique_instances[:]
    rng.shuffle(shuffled)
    split_idx = int(len(shuffled) * bank_fraction)
    return shuffled[split_idx:], shuffled[:split_idx]  # (corpus_pool, bank_pool)
```

Disjointness holds by construction because `corpus_pool` and `bank_pool` are a strict
partition of an already-deduplicated set — no instance can appear in both. The existing
`test_fewshot_bank_disjoint.py` 3-way gate (normalized text + canonical algebra +
skeleton + cosine ceiling) then runs unchanged as the **backstop** proving the property
holds, exactly as D-10 specifies.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **Deriving the gloss by asking an LLM to summarize the gold SPARQL:** reintroduces the
  SPARQL-literacy dependency D-09 exists to remove (Pattern 3, Alternatives Considered).
- **Generating one pool then filtering for disjointness post-hoc:** explicitly rejected
  by CONTEXT D-10 — "would thrash against the cosine-similarity ceiling at scale."
- **Maximizing discordant-pair proportion as a goal in itself:** as shown in Pattern 1,
  this is not what buys power — N and avoiding 0%/100%-pass-rate classes are what matter.
- **Adding ontology terms that overlap the vocabulary of an existing `expect_refusal`
  rationale** (e.g. "amount", "employment/salary/department") — see Pitfall 1.
- **Auto-regenerating `baseline.json` in CI** — this repo's established discipline (06.2,
  07) requires the fold-in to always be a manual, human-reviewed step; NL-GROW-07 must
  follow this exactly, not introduce an automated regeneration path.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Ontology extension can silently invalidate existing `expect_refusal` negatives
**What goes wrong:** `neg-absent-invoice-class`'s rationale asserts "the ontology has no
:Invoice class or amount property"; `neg-unexpressible-salary-relation`'s rationale
asserts "the schema models people, orders and acquaintance links — not employment,
salary or departments." If the ontology extension (D-07) introduces a `:Company`/
`:employedBy` edge, or an `:amount`-named attribute anywhere, these rationale texts
become factually stale even though the *test* (a hardcoded malformed/unparseable
`scripted:` string) still mechanically passes.
**Why it happens:** The `scripted:` field for these negatives is a deliberately
malformed SPARQL string (a drift-proof trigger, independent of the ontology) — so
nothing *fails* when the ontology changes, but the human-readable narrative silently
goes stale, which is exactly the kind of quiet drift the disjointness/parity gates in
this repo are designed to prevent elsewhere.
**How to avoid:** (1) Do NOT introduce `:Company`, `:employedBy`, `:salary`,
`:department`, `:Invoice`, or `:amount`-named terms in the extension — this research's
proposed extension (`:Product`/`:Category`/`:Review`) deliberately avoids this
vocabulary. (2) Add a Wave-0 test that greps each `expect_refusal` case's rationale text
against the extended ontology's declared local names and fails if an asserted-absent
term now exists.
**Warning signs:** A new class/property whose local name or domain overlaps a phrase in
an existing negative's `expected:` rationale string.

### Pitfall 2: Power formula misuse — treating "more discordant pairs" as a costless power gain
**What goes wrong:** Reading D-03 ("rebalance toward failing classes... for headroom")
as "concentrate on classes that fail 100% of the time" — but a class that fails under
EVERY arm (baseline AND intervention) contributes concordant-fail pairs, not discordant
ones, and adds zero information to McNemar's test.
**Why it happens:** Confusing "the corpus needs headroom" (pass rate meaningfully <1.0,
already an existing invariant) with "discordant pairs should be maximized" — the two are
related but not identical; see Pattern 1's worked explanation.
**How to avoid:** Target per-class baseline pass rates in the ~20-70% band, not classes
sitting at 0% or 100% under the anchor model. Verify this empirically against a fresh
live sweep once a representative subset of the grown corpus exists, rather than assuming
it from the class label alone.
**Warning signs:** A rebalanced corpus where a scripted/live sweep still shows an entire
difficulty class at 0% or 100% pass rate.

### Pitfall 3: Committing to a single static N without an empirical re-check
**What goes wrong:** Locking N=785 (or any single number) purely from the Pattern-1
sensitivity table's assumed π, then discovering after generation that the actual
observed discordant rate on the new corpus differs materially from the planning
assumption — the achieved MDE could still be >5pt even at the "right" N.
**Why it happens:** π is fundamentally unknown until real model behavior over the new,
harder corpus is observed; the Phase-07 data used for the planning assumption came from
the OLD 25-case corpus, not the new one.
**How to avoid:** Treat the target N as a first-wave commitment, not a final number.
Implement `achieved_mde(n, observed_pi, alpha, power)` (the inverse of the sizing
formula) as a committed, tested function; after the first live/scripted sweep on the
grown corpus, recompute achieved MDE against the OBSERVED π and grow further only if
still >5pt (per D-02's "no hard cap").
**Warning signs:** A plan that treats N as fixed-and-final before any real pass/fail
data exists on the new corpus.

### Pitfall 4: LLM faithfulness judge only checks one direction
**What goes wrong:** A faithfulness judge that only asks "does the question capture
what the gloss says" can pass a question that ADDS an unstated constraint (e.g. gloss
says "count orders per person," LLM question asks "count orders placed in the last
year per person") — the added detail changes the gold's actual scope but a
same-general-topic judge waves it through.
**Why it happens:** Single-direction faithfulness prompts are the common failure mode
in NL-generation-judge literature generally (not specific to this repo) — over-specific
or under-specific rephrasing reads as "close enough" to a lenient judge.
**How to avoid:** The LLM-judge prompt must explicitly ask two separate yes/no
questions: "(a) does the natural question ask for anything the gloss does NOT mention?
(b) does the gloss require anything the natural question drops?" Either "yes" fails the
pre-screen and routes to human review (or rejection).
**Warning signs:** A pre-screen pass-rate suspiciously close to 100% (implies the judge
isn't discriminating) — sanity-check by manually inspecting a sample of judge-passed
pairs during the first generation run.

### Pitfall 5: Growing the bank without growing the negative/refusal class proportionally
**What goes wrong:** D-03 explicitly calls out negatives as a class to over-weight, but
the few-shot bank (unlike the corpus) has no natural place for `expect_refusal`-style
examples — a "few-shot example of a refusal" doesn't fit the bank's `{question, query}`
shape (there's no gold *query* for a refusal).
**Why it happens:** The bank's schema (`FewShotIndex.from_corpus_files`) expects
`{question, query}` pairs, which structurally cannot represent "the correct answer is to
refuse." This is a real schema mismatch, not an oversight.
**How to avoid:** Confirm early (Wave 0) whether negatives belong ONLY in the corpus (not
the bank) — this is very likely correct given the bank's schema — and size the corpus's
negative-class proportion independently of the bank's proportional growth, rather than
assuming a matching ratio.
**Warning signs:** An attempt to force an `expect_refusal`-shaped entry into
`fewshot_bank.yml`'s `examples:` list.

## Code Examples

### Power-analysis helpers (proposed addition to `runner.py` or a new `power.py`)
```python
# Source: derived from Connor (1987) sample-size formula for McNemar's test,
# following the same normal-approximation convention used by
# powerandsamplesize.com / PASS / pwrss documentation of the Connor family.
# Mirrors this repo's existing pure-Python stats convention
# (runner.paired_mcnemar / bootstrap_paired_delta avoid scipy deliberately).
from __future__ import annotations
import math

# Standard normal quantiles for common alpha/power values — avoids a scipy
# dependency for the two-value lookup this formula needs.
_Z = {
    0.05: 1.9600,   # two-sided alpha=0.05  -> z_{alpha/2}
    0.10: 1.6449,   # two-sided alpha=0.10  -> z_{alpha/2}
}
_Z_BETA = {
    0.70: 0.5244,
    0.80: 0.8416,
    0.90: 1.2816,
}


def required_n(mde: float, discordant_rate: float, *, alpha: float = 0.05, power: float = 0.80) -> int:
    """Minimum paired-case count N to detect a paired pass-rate delta >= `mde`
    at the given `alpha` (two-sided) and `power`, assuming a discordant-pair
    proportion of `discordant_rate` (Connor 1987 approximate formula)."""
    z_a = _Z[alpha]
    z_b = _Z_BETA[power]
    n = ((z_a + z_b) ** 2) * discordant_rate / (mde ** 2)
    return math.ceil(n)


def achieved_mde(n: int, discordant_rate: float, *, alpha: float = 0.05, power: float = 0.80) -> float:
    """Inverse of `required_n` — the minimum detectable effect actually
    achieved at case count `n` and an (observed or assumed) discordant rate."""
    z_a = _Z[alpha]
    z_b = _Z_BETA[power]
    return math.sqrt(((z_a + z_b) ** 2) * discordant_rate / n)
```

## Security Domain

This phase touches test fixtures, a generator script, and gated LLM calls — it does not
add a network-facing surface, so most ASVS categories are not applicable. The relevant
subset:

### Applicable ASVS Categories

| ASVS Category | Applies | Standard Control |
|---------------|---------|-------------------|
| V2 Authentication | No | No new auth surface |
| V3 Session Management | No | No new session surface |
| V4 Access Control | No | No new access-control surface |
| V5 Input Validation | Yes | Generator-produced golds MUST pass the existing `CorpusCase` pydantic gate (gold-must-parse) before being written to `corpus.yml`/`fewshot_bank.yml` — same validation surface the harness already enforces, applied to machine-generated data instead of hand-authored data |
| V6 Cryptography | No | No new secrets/crypto surface introduced |

### Known Threat Patterns for this stack

| Pattern | STRIDE | Standard Mitigation |
|---------|--------|-----------------------|
| Secret leakage via the gated LLM-authoring step (D-06) | Information Disclosure | Reuse the existing `NL2SPARQL_API_KEY`-only convention (README §6: "never commit a key or raw prompts/completions... only aggregate numbers cross into `baseline.json`"); the generator's LLM calls must follow the identical discipline — outputs (questions/glosses) are trusted committed data, but raw API request/response logs must never be committed |
| A generated gold silently encoding a hallucinated/incorrect schema mapping smuggled into `corpus.yml` as "trusted repo data" | Tampering | The `test_gold_transpilable.py` gate + the new NL-GROW-02 idiom-compliance test are the mitigation — every generated gold is mechanically re-verified against the live resolver/transpiler before being treated as ground truth, not merely trusted because the generator produced it |
